### 87. The quality chips' stylesheet writes the chip box once

Upstream's `buildCss` in `js/tags/qualitytags.js` put `JE.core.tagRenderer.chipBaseCss(...)` (the chip box: padding,
font size, border, shadow and their `.layout-mobile`, width and container-query variants) in front of each of its colour
rules, so the stylesheet held the same box once per quality colour. Here the same code is `qualityCss` in
`client/src/features/media-tags/types/quality/quality-data.ts` (called by `qualitytags.ts`'s `buildCss`); it now writes
the box once, then the colour rules, as upstream does. No other difference from upstream.

- **Same looks.** The box declares no background or colour, and every colour rule (`.quality-overlay-container
  .quality-overlay-label[data-quality="…"]`) is more specific than it, so moving the box ahead of the colour rules changes
  no computed value; the rules around `${rules}` (low resolution, categories, editions) keep their order relative to the
  box. The lab check below shows every drawn chip's computed look unchanged.
- **Other writers of quality colours.** `layout/tag-styles.ts` (Tag Styles) writes per-label colour rules without a chip
  box; nothing else repeats `chipBaseCss` per rule (the language tags call it twice, for two selectors).
- **Nothing stored or cached changes**, and no cached-output version covers client stylesheets.
- **Test.** Upstream adds none. `tests/frontend/features/media-tags/features-quality-display.test.mjs` gains "the quality
  stylesheet writes the chip box once and one colour rule per quality": it injects the stylesheet as the tag pipeline
  does when the renderer registers, then counts the chip box (once), the colour rules (more than 50, including 4K,
  1080p, HDR10, ATMOS and LOW-RES with their background and colour) and the size (under 60,000 characters). It failed
  before the port (92 chip boxes) and passes on it (`p87.tests.txt`).
