## 2. The entry

### 96. Approving a Reclaimerr deletion request with its grace period, now, or on a chosen day

What upstream changed. Reclaimerr's approve call takes `scheduled_at` (omitted: its grace period, null: now, a future
moment: that day). The Leaving soon page's approve step offers that choice (Use Reclaimerr's grace period, with its days
when Reclaimerr says how many; Delete now; Choose a date, with a date-and-time picker that refuses a past moment) and sends
it as `when` (`default`, `now`, `date`) plus `date`. An older Reclaimerr ignores `scheduled_at` and would delete at once
whatever day was picked, so the choice is only offered, and a day or an explicit now only sent, when Reclaimerr's public
`/openapi.json` names the approve body that takes it (`ApproveDeleteRequest`); without it the approve call is the old one,
and a day or now asked for anyway is refused with nothing sent. The list says so (`canSchedule`, administrators only) and
names the grace period (`graceDays`, from Reclaimerr's general settings). Approved requests show the day they are deleted;
one whose schedule failed or is blocked stays on the page as a failed deletion; an approval reads the Leaving soon facts
again at once. The native menu's popup is painted from the theme so its options read on any theme. Not in the subject: a
request filed in Reclaimerr itself by an account the user map or Reclaimerr's Jellyfin link names (say `ATM_user`) now
shows that Jellyfin person.

Here. The server hunks apply to the moved files: `Features/MediaTags/Services/ReclaimerrActions.cs` (`ApprovalTiming`,
`SchedulingSupportedAsync`, `GracePeriodDaysAsync`, `ReviewRequestAsync` with the timing and the refresh after an approval,
`JellyfinNameOfAsync`, the two new row fields and the approved-list filter) and `ReclaimerrSession.cs`
(`MappedJellyfinUser`). The routes are partials of the feature's one controller: `canSchedule` and `graceDays` in
`JeMediaTagsController.LeavingSoon.cs` (`GetLeavingSoonList`), `when` and `date` in `JeMediaTagsController.Reclaimerr.cs`
(`ReviewReclaimerrRequest`); the body and DTO types are in `Features/MediaTags/Models/ReclaimerrRequests.cs`. The client is
re-implemented in `client/src/features/media-tags/leaving-soon/leaving-soon-page.ts` (shipped inside
`tags/tag-item-menu.js`); `listOf` reads the two new fields (anything but `true` is "cannot", a non-integer grace period is
unknown). Upstream's five `en.json` strings and its four docs pages apply as is.

Differences from upstream:

1. **The "can it schedule" answer is kept per Reclaimerr address.** Upstream keeps one answer for ten minutes (and the grace
   period for one) whatever the address: within ten minutes of a "yes" from a newer Reclaimerr, pointing the plugin at an
   older one still offers the date picker and sends `scheduled_at` to a Reclaimerr that deletes at once, the case the check
   exists for. Here both are kept with the address they were read from. Filed as a found bug (section 5).
2. **The list asks while it reads.** The scheduling question and the grace period are read while the two request lists are
   read, under the same budget; upstream asks first, which spends the lists' budget before they start. Unknown (the budget ran
   out) is "cannot", so the page offers the approve step it always had.
3. **The review keeps this tree's service-account note** (the administrator named in the note whenever the service account
   sends the review, from the 40738412 port); the day goes in both bodies.
4. **The person lookup runs under the list's budget like the title lookups**: one that runs out of it leaves that card
   without its person. In upstream the lookup's catch tests the token that is the budget, so a budget that runs out drops
   the whole list as "could not be read", the same fault as the title lookups (filed on the 40738412 sync).
