Spoiler Guard
blur_on
Spoiler Guard hides episode thumbnails, season posters, movie posters and chapter scenes for unwatched titles each user opts into. It also strips spoiler-bearing metadata: synopses, episode titles, tags, taglines, ratings, air dates, cast on unwatched episodes, chapter names, and TMDB / user reviews on opted-in series.
Turns the whole feature on. While it's off, the per-show toggle button never appears and nothing is ever hidden. Turn it on to let users protect individual shows, movies, and collections from their own detail pages.
How protected cards look. Show stock cards (default) swaps each unwatched image for a safe parent-level placeholder: episodes show their series backdrop, seasons show the series poster, collection-opted movies show the collection art. Blur images instead applies a strong blur to the original so silhouettes and dominant colours still hint at "something is here". Pick stock cards for the strictest hide; pick blur if the placeholders feel too aggressive.
How strong the blur is. 5 is barely smudged, 40 is the recommended default (characters become unrecognisable but the colours still come through), 100 is almost a solid colour wash. Applies to "Blur images" mode, and to "Show stock cards" mode when an item has no safe parent art to substitute (e.g. a directly-opted-in movie), where the original is blurred as a fallback.
By default the protection covers posters, episode thumbs, and screenshots, but leaves the wide cinematic backdrop / fanart on detail pages alone (it's usually atmosphere, not plot). Turn this on if you want backdrops hidden too for maximum protection.
When on, a protected movie's poster still shows in full, but the scene thumbnails inside the movie's detail page (chapters, screenshots) stay hidden. Useful if you find movie posters are usually marketing art that doesn't give plot away, while the chapter scene-thumbs inside the movie do. Doesn't affect TV shows.
When on, Spoiler Guard turns itself on for a series the moment a user starts Season 1 Episode 1 of a show they've never watched. They don't have to remember to flip the toggle before pressing play. Rewatches and jumping in at later episodes are ignored; only a fresh first-play counts.
When on, requesting something through Seerr also pre-arms Spoiler Guard for that title: when the show or movie lands in the library, protection is already on for the user who requested it. Users can also pre-arm titles manually with the "Enable Spoiler Guard" button in the Seerr details popup (always available regardless of this toggle), handy when someone else requested the title first.
After a user flips Spoiler Guard on or off, card images update on the page instantly. By default, on-page text (description, episode titles, ratings) only refreshes when they navigate next. Turn this on to also reload the whole page after a toggle so the text updates straight away, at the cost of a brief page flash. Most users prefer it off for the smoother feel.
Hide metadata on unwatched episodes
visibility_off
These options hide spoiler-bearing text on protected TV shows and their unwatched episodes. Hidden centrally so the protection works the same in every Jellyfin client.
Swaps the description on a protected TV show's own detail page for the placeholder text below. Independent from episode descriptions, so you can keep the show's general premise visible while still hiding episode plots.
Swaps the per-episode plot summary for the placeholder text below. The single biggest source of leaks, strongly recommended on.
Text shown where a protected TV show or episode description would normally appear, so the page doesn't have an empty gap. Default: "Spoiler Guard activated".
Hides plot-bearing tags (phrases like "Death of a main character") and the genre / quality / language / rating chips Jellyfin Enhanced draws on top of cards. Recommended on.
Chapter labels like "X dies" or "Y is revealed" can spoil the whole episode. Hides the names but leaves the chapter markers on the seek bar so users can still skip around. Recommended on.
Marketing taglines like "Everything changes tonight" basically advertise the twist. Recommended on.
Hides both the community and critic ratings on unwatched episodes. A standout score like "9.8/10" on a specific episode usually signals a major event. Recommended on.
A long gap before a particular episode can give away "this one's the season finale" without even reading the title. Hides the date so the schedule itself stays a surprise.
Swaps episode names like "The Death of X" for "Season 2, Episode 6". Some clients show the title in navigation tooltips and breadcrumbs where the swap can look a bit clinical; turn off if your users prefer to keep the original titles visible.
An unexpected guest star, or a regular suddenly missing from the cast list, can give away the whole episode. Hides the cast on unwatched episodes; pick what counts as "cast" below.
Guest stars only keeps the regular cast visible, since they're in every episode anyway and don't give anything away. Entire cast is the strict option for shows where the recurring cast appearing or not appearing in a given episode is itself the spoiler.
Hides the Reviews panel on the detail pages of shows the user has Spoiler Guard on for. Reviews very often spoil plot points from anywhere in the series. Recommended on.
Advanced per-category reveals
Instead of one uniform strip for every unwatched episode, the user's next unwatched episode and the rest of its season get the reveal settings below. Later seasons, skipped earlier seasons and specials always keep the full strip. A reveal only relaxes a strip that is enabled above; categories whose strip is off show everywhere already. Each user can opt back into the uniform full strip from their settings panel.
visibility
Checked = still hidden for that category. Unchecked = revealed. The next-episode title starts revealed; that's the point of advanced mode: see what you're about to watch, and nothing more.
"Current season" is the season containing the next unwatched episode. Card rating overlays and search results stay fully protected regardless of these reveals; only the episode metadata itself (lists and detail pages) is revealed.
